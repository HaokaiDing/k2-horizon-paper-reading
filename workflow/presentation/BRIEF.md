# Brief: 10-minute lab-meeting talk

**Title:** "Handheld-gripper data on other robot hands: where we stand, and what our lab should try first"

**Audience and goal.** Our robot-manipulation lab at MBZUAI (students and postdocs). The talk should leave them
knowing what FastUMI changed, why data from a handheld gripper does not simply run on a different robot hand, what
the field looks like in numbers, and it should end with a concrete ask: approve a 4-week pilot with three steps.

**Form.** 10 to 12 slides in 16:9. One message per slide, written as the slide title (a full short sentence).
At most about 45 words of text per slide. Structure:
1. Title slide (title, subtitle "Lab meeting, October 2026", presenter "Haokai Ding")
2. Opening: the problem in one slide (why handheld data matters and the embodiment gap in one line)
3-4. What FastUMI changed (hardware and tracking; data and results that matter for transfer)
5. What breaks when the end-effector differs
6. Approaches in the literature (grouped, one line each)
7-8. The field in numbers: at least one real chart drawn from Source 2 (e.g. papers per year, or handheld vs all
   robot-free share), plus the caveats in small text
9. Open problems
10. The ask: the 4-week pilot (what, how long, what we measure, what we need)
11. References slide (short form: first author or system name, year, arXiv ID or DOI) for every paper cited

**Rules.**
- Use only facts in the sources. Every number must come from the sources (or from this brief, e.g. the 4-week pilot).
- Mark claims with the short reference, e.g. "(FastUMI, 2024)" or a superscript [n] matching the references slide.
- No placeholders, no notes to the speaker on the slides, no lorem ipsum.
