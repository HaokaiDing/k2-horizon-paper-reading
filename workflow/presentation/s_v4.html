Handheld-gripper data on other robot hands: where we stand, and what our lab should try first

Lab meeting, October 2026

Haokai Ding

Handheld-gripper data is scalable, but the embodiment gap blocks direct reuse.

  • UMI-style interfaces separate teacher from robot and replay demos directly[2].
  • FastUMI scales this with hardware-independent collection[1].
  • Transfer fails when the executing end-effector differs from the handheld gripper[2][5].
Handheld-gripper data on other robot hands2

FastUMI standardizes the interface and replaces GoPro VIO with a RealSense T265.

Hardware

  • ISO-standard flange plates and adjustable camera mounts[1].
  • Plug-in fingertips for five common grippers[1].
  • Cover >90% of Open X-Embodiment grippers[1][3].

Tracking

  • RealSense T265 replaces GoPro VIO[1].
  • Eliminates complex calibration and transforms[1].
  • Caveat: T265 degrades under occlusion[1].
Handheld-gripper data on other robot hands3

FastUMI's 10k+ trajectories show depth and data scale improve transfer-relevant policies.

  • >10,000 demos, 22 tasks, 19 objects, 12 skills, ~6–12 s[1].
  • Depth raises success: Pick Lid 53.33→80.00%, Open Ricecooker 20.00→93.33%[1].
  • Single fisheye GoPro matches multi-view on Open Container[1].
Handheld-gripper data on other robot hands4

A different end-effector breaks geometry, actions, tracking, and mechanics.

Geometry & actions

  • Grasp geometry and contact points shift[5].
  • Diffusion Policy relative TCP poses do not handle embodiment[11].

Tracking & mechanics

  • T265 degrades under occlusion and low light[1].
  • Non-parallel jaws displace TCP; local-Z compensation[1].
Handheld-gripper data on other robot hands5

Four ways the literature approaches the gap

Pool across embodiments

  • Open X-Embodiment: RT-X on mixed robots beats single-robot models[3].
  • RT-1: adding Kuka data can help[8].

Retarget mismatched hands

  • Doshi et al.: compensate shape and kinematic differences[5].
  • Sanches et al.: wearable interfaces map human hand motion to robot hands[6].
  • Dexcap: mocap gloves retargeted to a dexterous hand via IK[7].

Fix policy and data

  • Diffusion Policy: relative TCP poses decide how much mismatch the policy sees[11].
  • Data Quality in Imitation Learning: noise and bias affect downstream policies[12].

Useful contrasts

  • UMI: assumes the robot wears the same fingers[2].
  • Human video: third-person demos, the far end of the gap[4].
  • VR teleoperation: collects on the target robot, no gap[9].
  • Grasping in the Wild: early handheld grabber, transfer not its focus[10].

Handheld-gripper data on other robot hands7

Handheld grippers are robot-free twice as often as the field (83.9% vs 41.4%).

Only 27 papers state dataset size (median 2,100 demos, range 3–1,200,000); 108 abstracts do not specify an end-effector. Labels were produced by a language model from abstracts (relevance spot check 19/20); some dexterous-hand devices are labelled other_teleop, so the handheld count of 31 is a lower bound[14].

Open problems: data quality, unsupported grippers, mismatched evaluation, sensing, and policy-first fixes.

  • Clean and re-annotate occluded or close-range handheld recordings[1][12].
  • Build a retargeting baseline for one gripper outside FastUMI's five attachments[1][5][6][7].
  • Evaluate transfer on mismatched end-effectors with per-task success rates[3][8][14].
  • Test tactile/force sensing, wireless transfer, and mobile-manipulator morphologies[1].
  • Try policy-level fixes first: depth-enhanced Diffusion Policy and more data[1][11].
