# Brief: short technical review

**Title:** "Moving handheld-gripper demonstrations to other robot hands: where the field stands (October 2026)"

**Reader.** New members of my lab (robot manipulation, MBZUAI) who will work on UMI-style data collection and need
a 10-minute orientation before reading papers.

**Length and form.** 1,400 to 1,900 words, Markdown, with these sections:
1. Summary (4 to 5 sentences)
2. Why handheld-gripper data, and what FastUMI changed (hardware, tracking, data, results, using Source A)
3. The embodiment gap: what breaks when the robot's end-effector differs from the handheld gripper (Sources A, B)
4. Approaches in the literature, grouped by theme as in Source B, each paper with one or two sentences on how it
   bears on the gap
5. What the numbers say about the field (Source C: method shares, growth, robot-free share, dataset release,
   handheld vs field robot-free contrast), with the caveats stated there
6. Open problems and what a newcomer should try first (3 to 5 concrete points that follow from sections 2 to 5)
7. References

**Rules.**
- Use only facts in `SOURCES.md`. Every number must come from the sources; do not add numbers.
- Cite as [n] in the text; the reference list gives for every entry: authors, title, year, and its arXiv ID or DOI
  with a link, exactly as given in the sources. Cite FastUMI as well. Cite nothing that is not in the sources.
- Finished text: no placeholders, no "[TODO]", no notes to the writer, no "as an AI".
